v1/ JavaFX 26/ AtlantaFX + modula.css/ 2026-08-08

Modula is a radio, not an SDR panel — so this kit specifies a listening device, not a dashboard. One amber number is the brightest thing on the screen; everything else is instrumentation around it, dimmed until it has something to say. Every value shown below is one the receiver actually reports.

01

Foundations

The palette has one idea behind it: the dial is emitting light and everything else is reflecting it. That is why the ground is a cool-neutral near-black rather than a warm charcoal — a warm ground would put the amber in the surface, and the amber has to read as the only lit thing. Daylight inverts the ground but keeps the same rule: the frequency is the one saturated mark on the page.

Amber is a budget, not a palette. If a second thing turns amber, the frequency stops being the brightest mark and the display becomes a panel of equals — which is the exact failure mode the product brief rules out. Lock is teal, faults are coral, everything else is ink at one of three weights.

Type

One face does the instrument work: IBM Plex Mono, drawn for IBM's technical products, so its digits read as a readout rather than as code. It is the only face allowed to show a number. Prose — this document, tooltips, error text — is the platform sans.

The dial

Everything the receiver knows lives on one inset panel — the glass — in a fixed reading order: where you are, who it is, what they're playing, how well it's coming in. Nothing in the glass is interactive. That separation is the whole layout idea: the glass reports, the body below it acts.

04

Signal & lock

The receiver measures signal two ways and they mean opposite things. The kit shows one and hides the other, because only one of them is about listening.

MeasurementRange seenDirectionShown as
signalDbfs
RF power
 −60 … 0 higher = stronger The meter and the number beside it
noiseDbfs
multiplex noise
 −5.8 empty
−51 strong		 lower = stronger Never. It drives seek, and a meter that fell as reception improved would be unreadable.
Why the meter is honest but not useful for tuning. With the dongle's AGC on, an empty channel is gained up until its RF power reads much like an occupied one — measured −9.85 dBFS empty against −9.75 for a weak station. So the meter tells you how hard the front end is working, not whether anything is there. Seek is what answers that, from multiplex noise, thresholded at −20 dBFS. Do not add a signal-strength squelch to the UI; it would be measuring the wrong thing.

Meter anatomy

Mono — amber end to end
−31 dBFS
Stereo — tip resolves to teal
−7 dBFS
Weak — no RDS at this level
−49 dBFS

The bar maps −60…0 dBFS onto its width, which is what RadioPane already does. The one addition is that the last third of the gradient resolves toward lock teal only while the pilot is locked — so signal strength and stereo, two facts a listener conflates anyway, are carried by one mark.

STEREO reports the transmitter, not your setting. Forcing mono with the checkbox must not unlight it; the station is still sending a pilot. That distinction is already correct in setPilotLocked and is easy to lose.

Pilot lock is a product-visible delay. At the shipped 50 Hz loop bandwidth the PLL locks within about 100 ms of a retune. That is fast enough to animate the badge on rather than pop it — a 120 ms fade reads as the indicator warming up, which is what a real one does.

TA is the one case where amber is allowed to leave the dial. A traffic announcement is the single event in this product important enough to outrank the frequency, and every car radio in existence signals it. It is also the only one — adding a second exception is how the accent budget in §01 gets spent.
06

Presets

Today presets are a ListView with a name field, a Save button and a Remove button — four controls and 150 px of vertical space for something a car radio does with one row of buttons. The kit replaces it with a chip row: one tap tunes, the current station reads as selected, and saving is the trailing +.

  • ·Selected = tuned. The chip matching the current frequency is outlined amber, which is also what makes Save redundant — Presets.contains already drives that today.
  • ·Unnamed presets show an em dash, not an empty chip, so the row keeps its rhythm. Preset.label() already falls back to the frequency alone.
  • ·Naming happens after saving, not before. + stores the frequency immediately and offers the name inline; a name field standing empty next to a Save button asks a question before you've decided to keep the station.
  • ·Remove is on the chip — long-press, or Delete with it focused. A dedicated Remove button that is disabled most of the time is a control earning its keep 5% of the time.
The row scrolls horizontally and never wraps, so the window height is fixed no matter how many presets exist. ~/.modula/presets.txt stays hand-editable and unchanged — this is a view change only.
07

Controls

Below the glass, in one row: the tuning rocker, then power. Everything else — volume, region, forced mono — is secondary and sits in a footer bar at the bottom edge, because none of it is touched while listening.

VOL EUROPE STEREO ON
−16 dBFS · sync 98% · 0 dropped
A real finding: the arrow glyphs are not in the font. The buttons are labelled ◀◀ ◀ ▶ ▶▶ and ★ — U+25C0, U+25B6, U+2605. Those are absent from most UI and mono faces, so today they render from whatever fallback each OS happens to pick, at whatever size and baseline that face uses. Draw them as shapes instead — an SVGPath triangle, or a CSS border triangle as above. Identical on every platform, and they take the button's text colour for free.

08

Spectrum strip

Milestone 5 calls for a spectrum strip — ±600 kHz, and explicitly not a waterfall. It is not built yet, so this is a specification rather than a reskin. The job it does is narrow: show that the neighbours exist, and that you are centred on one of them.

  • ·Span is fixed at the sample rate — 1.2 MHz, which is ±600 kHz. No zoom, no span control: it is a fact of the front end, not a setting.
  • ·The centre marker is amber and everything else is ink. This is the one place the accent leaves the dial, because the mark means "the frequency you are reading above".
  • ·Channel grid, not a frequency axis. Ticks land on BandPlan grid points, so a neighbour reads as "the next station up" rather than as an offset in kHz.
  • ·Filled area, one hairline. A stroke-only trace on a dark ground reads as noise; a filled area under a 1 px line reads as a shape at a glance.
  • ·No waterfall, no dB axis, no peak hold. Each is a panel feature. If the answer to "would a car radio have it" is no, the strip does not get it.
  • ·Redraw at status cadence, coalesced the same way onStatus already is — at most one pending repaint, on a Canvas, never per FFT frame.
It also makes seek legible. During a seek the strip is the one surface where the sweep is visible as motion across the band — which is exactly why §03 dims the dial and lights the cursor. Build them together; each is half of the same feedback.

10

Adoption

Everything above documents surfaces that already exist, reskinned. These three are the actual changes, ordered so each is worth landing on its own — and none of them touches dsp, demod, band, radio or config. The layering rule holds: this is all ui.

01

A stylesheet at all

Modula has no CSS today — the look is a dozen inline setStyle calls with hardcoded pixel sizes, so there is nowhere to put a theme and no way to have two. Add AtlantaFX as the user-agent stylesheet so the five standard controls in use (ComboBox, Slider, ListView, CheckBox, ProgressBar) come themed, then layer modula.css for the parts AtlantaFX has never heard of: the glass, the dial, the meter gradient, the band scale, the preset chips.

Night and day become two token blocks in one sheet, selected by a looked-up colour set — the same mechanism Editora uses, so there is precedent to copy rather than invent. Every setStyle call becomes a style class, which is also what makes the state language in §03 expressible: a state is a pseudo-class on the glass, not seven branches of string concatenation.

pom.xml — add atlantafx-base ModulaApp.start — setUserAgentStylesheet, attach modula.css RadioPane — delete every setStyle, add style classes resources/com/modula/modula.css — new
02

The glass, and the shell around it

Restructure the nine centred rows into the three bands in §09, with everything the receiver reports inside one inset panel and everything that acts below it. This is the change that makes it read as a radio, and it is mostly a reparenting job — the frequency, station, radio text, meter and STEREO labels all already exist and keep their update paths.

Do the preset chip row and type-to-tune here too: together they remove two rows and four controls, which is what buys the glass its vertical space at 520 px. Add the TP/TA badges while the glass is open — StationInfo has carried those two booleans all along and nothing has ever shown them.

RadioPane is 507 lines and its own comment says to move to FXML once the spectrum strip lands. This is that moment: split the glass into its own node so the strip has somewhere to go, and the state mapping has one owner.

ui/RadioPane — three bands; buildStationInfo/buildMeter fold into a glass node ui/GlassPane — new; owns the report surfaces and the state mapping ui/PresetBar — new; replaces the ListView block
03

One state function, not seven branches

applyStatus currently sets a progress value, a style string, two labels and a status message inline, and the seven states in §03 are implied by the order of those statements rather than named anywhere. Make the state explicit: a pure function from Status to an enum, in its own class, unit-tested — which fits Modula's own convention that anything expressible as a function belongs outside the UI with a test.

It is worth doing because the tricky cases are decisions, not drawing: STEREO must report the transmitter and not the user's forced-mono checkbox; a fault must hold the last frequency rather than blank it; seeking must dim the dial while droppedSamples > 0 must not. Each of those is a line in a test and an afternoon of confusion if it regresses.

ui/ReceiverState — new; enum + of(Status, boolean forcedMono), pure test/ui/ReceiverStateTest — new; one case per state and per trap above ui/GlassPane — applies a style class per state; no style strings
What this kit deliberately does not add. No demodulator picker, no bandwidth slider, no gain or AGC panel, no FFT settings, no squelch control, no dB axis, no waterfall — and no signal-strength squelch, which §04 shows would be measuring the wrong quantity. The product test stands: a preset button, yes; a decimation-stages spinner, no.
